# Lesson 01: Navigating Classroom Conflict

**Audience:** Novice preschool teachers. Oliver, Emma, and the classroom events are fictional. Illustrations are representational. Learning outcomes have not been evaluated with learners.

| Objective | Learning activity and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Distinguish facts from assumptions | Observe an ambiguous conflict, then classify five statements and review explanatory feedback. Learners can retry; a perfect score is not required. |
| Compare responses in context | Choose a response, optionally predict its outcome, observe one possible consequence, and review supports and trade-offs. Alternate branches let learners compare without replacing the original decision. |
| Justify and adapt a response | Interpret the colleague's new information, choose a revised approach, optionally explain it, and connect the decision to future teaching in reflection. |

## Design rationale

- **Observation before intervention:** Classifying claims and observations separates evidence from inferred intention before the learner has to make a judgment.
- **Experience before explanation:** Prediction invites the learner to articulate a mental model before comparing it with an illustrative consequence. Feedback then explains what the approach supports and when it may be insufficient, without presenting contextual decisions as right or wrong.
- **Adaptation:** All three paths converge at the colleague's account. The new evidence makes it possible to reconsider both perspectives. Keeping or changing an approach can be appropriate; reasoning and children's safety, dignity, and developmental needs matter.
- **Transfer:** The personalized review and optional, private reflections make earlier thinking visible and ask the learner what they might do in future practice.

## Branching and completion

Each first decision selects an outcome and rationale from structured content. The outcome is reviewed before its feedback. Alternate strategies preserve the initial choice and prediction. A confirmed initial change resets branch-dependent work and checks, not reflection notes. The revised response has its own feedback. Completion requires all required reviews and explicit confirmation; optional writing does not block progress. Responses are in browser memory, not sent or persisted across reloads.

## Planned evaluation

Pilot with novice preschool teachers. Observe classification misconceptions, whether learners use the new information in their explanations, whether the feedback helps them compare approaches, and whether transfer reflections name concrete classroom practices. Check keyboard navigation, comprehension, and mobile usability. This prototype does not measure professional mastery.
